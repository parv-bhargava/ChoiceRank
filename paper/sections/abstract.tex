Typed decision models return probabilities over caller-defined options without generating free text.
We evaluate four model families (Jev, Clef, Jeff, and Laya) as pointwise passage rerankers on the BM25 top-100 candidates of TREC DL 2019--2023 and eight BEIR collections, and ask three questions: how well they rank, whether their relevance probabilities are calibrated, and whether those probabilities help select context for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).
Across its three question formats, Jev averages 55.3--56.1 nDCG@10, on par with Qwen3-Reranker-8B (56.0) and above bge-reranker-v2-m3 (53.7), with large variation across collections.
Where the base model is known, same-base controls give mixed answers: Clef matches its base model on the three collections tested, while the small Jeff model improves on its base; Jev's base model is not public.
Without any target-collection labels, Jev's raw yes/no probabilities have lower calibration error than bge-reranker-v2-m3 (ECE 15.0 vs.\ 20.0), but calibrating bge on other collections with the same relevance threshold closes this gap (6.9 vs.\ 6.8).
In RAG on MuSiQue and NQ with three generators, Jev's rankings improve multi-hop answer F1 over bge-reranker-v2-m3; on the 329 MuSiQue questions that share no decomposition sub-question with the design splits, top-1 and Adaptive-$k$ gains of 10.7--18.8 F1 hold for every generator.
Probability thresholds, however, do not improve over fixed top-$k$ selection.
Typed decision models are thus competitive rerankers, but better-calibrated probabilities alone do not yield better context selection.
