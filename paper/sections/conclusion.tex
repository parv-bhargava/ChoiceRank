\section{Threats to Validity}

\paragraph{Pre-specified versus post-hoc analyses.}
Several analyses were added or changed after results were observed, and we flag them here in one place. (i)~The per-collection strict/lenient readout policy was introduced after observing a Choice-3 ECE of 43.9 on TREC-COVID; we therefore lead with the policy-free Noul readout and the strict-everywhere readout, and give both readouts for every collection (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:readouts}). (ii)~The $\pm2$ nDCG and $\pm2$ F1 equivalence margins, the TOST analyses, and the modified Knapp--Hartung sensitivity analysis are post-hoc and exploratory. (iii)~In RQ3, threshold-rule comparisons against top-3 and Adaptive-$k$ were fixed after the pilot; comparisons against top-2 and top-5 and the raw (non-Platt) thresholds were added after final results. (iv)~The 329-question MuSiQue subset is a retrospective sensitivity analysis on existing outputs, not a new confirmatory split.

\paragraph{Statistical dependence.}
The three question formats of each decision model are answered in one call per pair and are not independent samples. Holm correction is applied within each system--baseline pair across collections, not across the roughly 40 systems and formats. MuSiQue questions were split by ID, and 671 of 1{,}000 final questions share at least one decomposition sub-question with the calibration or pilot splits; question-level RAG tests ignore this dependence, which the family bootstrap on the clean subset addresses only partially.

\paragraph{Configurations rather than isolated effects.}
Same-base comparisons differ in prompts and context budgets (4{,}000 characters for Jev, Clef, and Jeff; 448 tokens for the label-probability controls), so they compare configurations rather than isolate decision-model training. Qwen3-Reranker-4B/8B use 4-bit weights. Clef and Qwen3-235B cover subsets of collections, and the Clef base control covers three. Each format uses a single prompt. Latencies come from different hardware and serving stacks.

\paragraph{Benchmarks and contamination.}
Our main experiments use BM25 candidates; on SPLADE++ candidates Jev trails the cross-encoders on DL19 but not on DL20 (Appendix~\ref{app:first-stage}). Calibration is measured on judged candidates, and numeric-threshold transfer groups need not share identical relevance semantics. We retain the DL23 synthetic queries and DL22 propagated judgments. Jev is a closed model whose size, training data, and cutoff are unknown; Robust04, MuSiQue, NovelEval, and FutureQueryEval all predate its release, so contamination cannot be excluded, and the answer-masking perturbation cannot separate reasoning from memorization. The RAG comparison covers bge-reranker-v2-m3 only, thresholds use target calibration labels, and the generator cannot abstain.

\section{Conclusion}

Large typed decision models are competitive pointwise passage rerankers: Jev is on par with Qwen3-Reranker-8B and ahead of bge-reranker-v2-m3 on average, with large variation across collections, and its gains over bge persist at equal passage budgets on News and Robust04. Same-base controls suggest that much of this effectiveness comes from the underlying LLM: Clef matches its own base on the three collections tested, while the small Jeff model improves on its base.
Decision-model probabilities are better calibrated than raw cross-encoder scores out of the box, but a cross-encoder calibrated on other collections with the same relevance threshold reaches similar error; their practical advantage is that no labelled source data are needed. Small open decision models remain weak deep rerankers under standard evaluation.
For RAG, Jev improves multi-hop answer quality over bge with all three generators, and it more often ranks the answer-bearing final hop first; on MuSiQue questions that share no components with the design splits, the top-1 and Adaptive-$k$ gains remain large ($+10.7$ to $+18.8$ F1). Thresholding its probabilities, however, does not improve over fixed top-$k$ selection. Calibration alone, in short, does not make context selection a simple threshold.
Future work should use family-disjoint RAG splits, add the strongest label-probability rerankers to the downstream comparison, and match context budgets across models.
