# ChoiceRank review - 10 October 2026

The experiments support a useful empirical paper. The strongest story is that typed decision models can be competitive passage rankers, while their probability interface has conditional value: better raw calibration does not automatically improve RAG context selection. I would revise the headline interpretations before submission. The most important changes are claim corrections and sensitivity analyses using existing outputs.

I reviewed the current 11-page [manuscript](C:/Users/parvb/OneDrive/Desktop/Research/ChoiceRank/paper/main.pdf), its LaTeX source, analysis code, saved ranking results, and RAG outputs. Separate reviews covered retrieval, calibration/statistics, and RAG. This was an audit of existing work; no paid model calls were made and the manuscript and experiment files were left unchanged. Selected external references were checked, rather than the entire bibliography.

What works well: the 13-collection evaluation, Qwen3-Reranker-4B/8B baselines, same-base comparisons, equal-context checks, calibration transfer analysis, and three-generator RAG study give the work substance. The negative threshold result is a contribution. The disclosures about post-hoc choices, missing judgments, and API uncertainty improve credibility. RAG selections and F1 were recomputed from saved answers for all six final dataset-generator runs with zero discrepancies. Each run contains all 1,000 question IDs under all 24 stored policies, without duplicate question-policy rows or missing answers. Calibration/pilot/final IDs are disjoint; leave-one-collection-out transfer excludes target labels; in-collection calibration actually splits by query.

1. **High priority: the raw RAG thresholds are not label-free.**

   [rag_generate.py:67](C:/Users/parvb/OneDrive/Desktop/Research/ChoiceRank/scripts/rag_generate.py:67) chooses the raw probability column by its Brier score on labelled target-dataset calibration questions. MuSiQue uses Noul and NQ uses C3 strict. Removing Platt scaling does not remove that label-dependent choice. The label-free description in the results, conclusion, and appendix therefore needs correction. This is label use on a separate calibration split, not final-test leakage.

   **Change:** describe these as "raw-probability thresholds using a calibration-selected readout." To make a genuinely label-free RAG claim, fix the readout independently of the target labels and evaluate that policy. The RQ2 raw Noul calibration result can still be described as requiring no target-label fitting.

2. **High priority: gold-answer masking creates an answer-location cue.**

   [rag_score.py:93](C:/Users/parvb/OneDrive/Desktop/Research/ChoiceRank/scripts/rag_score.py:93) replaces gold-answer occurrences with the distinctive string `[MASKED]`. In the final MuSiQue set, 999/1,000 last-hop paragraphs contain this marker, whereas only 1,621/19,988 candidate paragraphs contain it. The intervention removes answer text but reveals where the answer occurred.

   Consequently, the residual 43.8% last-hop preference does not establish that Jev identifies the passage resolving the question independently of answer-location cues or memorized chains. The sentence attributing "most of the preference" to question resolution in [results.tex:62](C:/Users/parvb/OneDrive/Desktop/Research/ChoiceRank/paper/sections/results.tex:62) is too strong.

   **Change:** retain the numbers as a descriptive robustness check and explain the marker confound. A stronger mechanism experiment would use consistent entity pseudonyms or a matched intervention that also transforms comparable non-answer entities. Apply it without a unique gold-answer marker. The observed unmasked preference and the large RAG gain over bge remain valuable results.

3. **High priority: formal pooled superiority/equivalence is fragile.**

   [review3_analyses.py:197](C:/Users/parvb/OneDrive/Desktop/Research/ChoiceRank/scripts/review3_analyses.py:197) uses DerSimonian-Laird random effects with normal confidence intervals; the equivalence calculation also uses normal quantiles. Two independent recalculations using the same cached query results and heterogeneity estimates found the following small-sample sensitivities:

   | Comparison | Reported inference | Hartung-Knapp / modified Knapp-Hartung sensitivity |
   |---|---|---|
   | Jev S4 - bge, 13 collections | 95% CI [0.07, 5.13] | [-0.37, 5.57] |
   | Jev S4 - bge, without Robust04 | 95% CI [0.09, 3.36] | [-0.42, 3.87] |
   | Jev S4 - Qwen8B, 13 collections | 90% CI [-1.26, 1.89], within +/-2 | [-1.40, 2.02], crosses the margin |
   | Clef S4 - its base, 3 collections | 90% CI [-1.22, 1.28], within +/-2 | approximately [-2.20, 2.25] |

   These are sensitivity results, not evidence that the saved averages or collection-level paired tests are wrong. Similar mean performance is robust; the formal pooled claims depend on inference choices and the post-hoc margin. Small-sample inference is particularly relevant with only three collections in the Clef/base comparison. [Rover, Knapp, and Friede (2015)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12874-015-0091-1) discuss this issue and modified inference for few studies with unequal precision.

   **Change:** add the sensitivity analysis, identify pooled equivalence as exploratory, and lead the abstract with the observed averages: Jev S4 56.1 versus Qwen8B 56.0. Explain the difference between the unweighted benchmark average and the random-effects pooled estimate. A forest plot would show the large collection heterogeneity more clearly than a single average.

4. **High priority: same-base comparisons do not isolate training alone.**

   Decision models receive up to 4,000 characters; base-model controls receive 448 XLM-R passage tokens. The prompts and answer interfaces also differ. These conditions are described in [experiments.tex:13](C:/Users/parvb/OneDrive/Desktop/Research/ChoiceRank/paper/sections/experiments.tex:13), but the abstract, introduction, and results make stronger causal claims.

   Jeff's approximately +7.5 nDCG improvement is a comparison of evaluated configurations, not an isolated training effect. Its improvements on short TREC passages are encouraging. Clef's base is already competitive on the three tested collections; this does not establish that decision training adds no ranking quality generally, especially given the inference sensitivity above.

   **Change:** write "Jeff outperforms its base under the tested configurations" and "we find no measurable ranking benefit for Clef over its base on the three tested collections." To isolate training, use identical passage text, matched relevance criteria, and comparable probability readouts. Qualify the introduction's claim that these controls "isolate" training.

5. **Medium priority: several interpretations go beyond the evaluated conditions.**

   Condensed nDCG and Judged@10 demonstrate sensitivity to incomplete judgments. They do not establish whether the excluded passages were relevant. In [results.tex:19](C:/Users/parvb/OneDrive/Desktop/Research/ChoiceRank/paper/sections/results.tex:19), "the same [pooling] effect" and the claim that Jev's DL tie is "conservative" should become uncertainty statements. Removing genuinely irrelevant unjudged passages can also improve condensed nDCG. A blinded sample of previously unjudged disagreement passages would provide direct evidence, if further assessment is feasible.

   The equal-context ablation supports Jev versus bge on three long-document collections. It does not establish pooled equal-context equivalence to Qwen8B; separate those claims in the conclusion. "Model capacity matters far more than question design" should become "differences between backends exceed differences among the three tested formats," because capacity is confounded with architecture, training, context, and Jev's unknown size.

   NovelEval and FutureQueryEval are useful additional benchmarks, but calling them "post-cutoff checks" is unsupported without the evaluated models' training cutoffs. Both precede Jev's release. The [FutureQueryEval paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16757) was submitted in August 2025. Call them newer-content benchmark checks and preserve the existing contamination caveat.

6. **Medium priority: align the RAG comparator with the strongest ranking comparator.**

   The ranking study identifies Qwen3-Reranker-8B as the strongest open pointwise baseline. The full RAG study compares Jev with bge, while only Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B appears in the mechanism check. The large MuSiQue gains are well supported specifically against bge; they do not establish an advantage over the strongest open reranker.

   **Change:** scope all RAG quality claims to bge. If adding one substantive experiment, first score MuSiQue with Qwen8B and report generator-free last-hop@1 and selection quality. A small fixed-policy comparison with one generator would then be the most informative downstream extension. Further threshold sweeps have lower value for this draft.

**A suggested abstract direction:**

Typed decision models return probabilities over caller-defined options without generating free text. We evaluate four model families as pointwise passage rerankers on the BM25 top-100 candidates of TREC DL 2019-2023 and eight BEIR collections, examining ranking effectiveness, relevance calibration, and RAG context selection. Jev's best format averages 56.1 nDCG@10, close to Qwen3-Reranker-8B at 56.0 and above bge-reranker-v2-m3 at 53.7, with substantial variation across collections. Same-base comparisons show that Clef's base model is already competitive on three tested collections, while Jeff outperforms its base under the evaluated configurations. On judged pairs from nine collections, Jev's raw yes/no probabilities have approximately five points lower mean ECE than bge; transfer-calibrated bge achieves similar mean ECE when source collections are grouped by relevance threshold. In a held-out RAG study on MuSiQue and NQ with three generators, Jev improves multi-hop answer quality over bge and more often ranks the final supporting passage first. Probability thresholds do not improve over fixed top-k selection. These findings establish typed decision models as competitive reranking configurations while showing that probability calibration alone does not guarantee better context selection.

I would address items 1-4 first, then correct scope and numerical wording, and complete the reproduction instructions. The paper's existing results can support a strong, more precise account without expanding every experimental axis.
