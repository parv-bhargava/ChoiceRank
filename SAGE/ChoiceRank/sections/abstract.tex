Typed decision models return probabilities over caller-defined options instead of free text.
We evaluate four model families as pointwise passage rerankers on TREC Deep Learning 2019--2023 and eight BEIR collections, asking how well they rank, whether their relevance probabilities are calibrated, and whether those probabilities help select context for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).
The strongest, Jev, averages 55.3--56.1 nDCG@10, close to a 4-bit Qwen3-Reranker-8B (56.0) and above bge-reranker-v2-m3 (53.7).
Without target-collection labels, its raw probabilities are better calibrated than bge-reranker-v2-m3's (expected calibration error 15.0 vs.\ 20.0), but calibrating the cross-encoder on other collections closes this gap, and rewording the prompt changes calibration error by more.
In RAG on MuSiQue with three generators, Jev's rankings give higher answer F1 than bge-reranker-v2-m3 in all 27 policy--generator comparisons and than Qwen3-Reranker-8B in 26, yet probability thresholds do not beat fixed top-$k$ selection.
Better-calibrated probabilities alone do not improve context selection.
