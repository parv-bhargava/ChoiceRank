\subsection{RAG prompt}
System message: ``Answer the question. Give only the short answer (a few words), with no explanation.'' User message: ``Answer the question based on the given passages. Give only the short answer (a few words), with no explanation.'' followed by a blank line, the selected passages in rank order (each as ``Passage $i$ (Title: \dots): \dots'', separated by blank lines), a blank line, and ``Question: \{question\}\textbackslash nAnswer:''. When a policy selects no passage, the user message is only ``Question: \{question\}\textbackslash nAnswer:''.

\subsection{Yes/no prompt}
System: ``You are a search relevance assessor.'' User: ``Query: \{query\}\textbackslash nPassage: \{passage\}\textbackslash n\textbackslash nDoes the passage answer the query? Answer with Yes or No only.''

\subsection{RQ3 details}\label{app:extra}
Generators were called through OpenRouter on 2026-10-04 by model name (\url{qwen/qwen-2.5-7b-instruct}, \url{meta-llama/llama-3.1-8b-instruct}, \url{qwen/qwen3-30b-a3b-instruct-2507}); OpenRouter does not expose weight snapshots. Qwen2.5-7B was served by one provider (Phala); Llama-3.1-8B by DeepInfra (61--64\% of calls), Novita (25--28\%), Groq (10\%), and Cloudflare (1\%); Qwen3-30B-A3B by six providers (StreamLake 31--32\%, SiliconFlow 24\%, Nebius 21--22\%, Alibaba 11\%, PrimeIntellect 11\%, DekaLLM 1--2\%).
Jev's probability readout was chosen by Brier score on the calibration split (MuSiQue: Choice-3 strict 0.110, lenient 0.099, Noul 0.094 $\rightarrow$ Noul; NQ: 0.122, 0.286, 0.143 $\rightarrow$ Choice-3 strict; family-disjoint MuSiQue calibration split: 0.081, 0.080, 0.068 $\rightarrow$ Noul). Platt parameters $(a,b)$ for $\sigma(a\,\mathrm{logit}(p)+b)$: Jev $(0.93, 0.60)$ and bge-reranker-v2-m3 $(0.42, 0.00)$ on MuSiQue; Jev $(1.01, 0.35)$ and bge-reranker-v2-m3 $(0.49, -0.01)$ on the family-disjoint MuSiQue split; Jev $(0.17, -0.77)$ and bge-reranker-v2-m3 $(0.34, -1.75)$ on NQ. Family-disjoint generations were run on 2026-10-17 with the same generator model names and prompt. Jev was called once per pair with default decoding. Its outputs are not fully deterministic: re-scoring DL19 and SciFact with the same snapshot in all three formats left 25--86\% of pairs identical, changed probabilities by 0.0004--0.010 on average (at most 0.21), and gave a mean per-query Kendall $\tau$ of 0.92--0.96 between runs; nDCG@10 changed by at most 0.9 points on DL19 and 0.3 on SciFact. Complete SHA-256 hashes for the MuSiQue and DPR input files are recorded in the reproduction manifest.

\subsection{Raw-probability thresholds (post-hoc)}
These avoid Platt fitting but retain the readout chosen by Brier score on labelled calibration questions. At $t=0.3/0.5/0.7$, Jev selects on average 1.4/0.8/0.4 passages on MuSiQue (empty for 25/46/70\%) and 3.1/2.5/1.9 on NQ (19/25/33\%). On MuSiQue, raw $P>0.3$ is 1.6--2.4 F1 below the Platt threshold and 3.4--5.9 below top-3 (all generators, $p<0.02$ except Llama versus Platt, $p=0.07$); on NQ it is within 1.3 F1 of both (only Qwen2.5-7B versus Platt is significant, $+0.8$). These are not label-free policies.

\subsection{Prompt sensitivity}\label{app:prompts}

We fixed three paraphrases each of the Noul question, the Qwen3-235B yes/no prompt, and the Qwen3-Reranker instruction before running any of them (code: \texttt{PARAPHRASES}, \texttt{USER\_PARAPHRASES}, \texttt{INSTRUCTIONS}). For Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B on DL19, DL20, and SciFact, the paraphrases change nDCG@10 by at most 2.0 points (DL19 69.4 with the default instruction versus 71.2--71.4), but they lower raw ECE on DL from 39.0 and 46.2 to 23.5--26.0 and 28.8--32.5; on SciFact ECE stays within 3.0--4.4. The default instruction is therefore the least calibrated of the four wordings, so raw-ECE comparisons with the Qwen rerankers depend on the prompt. Jev's Noul question is equally robust in ranking (nDCG@10 within 2.1 points of the original on all three collections) but not in calibration: raw ECE ranges from 7.5 to 25.9 on DL19 (original 12.4), 14.3 to 34.5 on DL20 (20.7), and 2.0 to 6.5 on SciFact (3.1). The original wording sits in the middle of this range, and the most direct wording (``Is the passage relevant to the search query?'') is the least calibrated on every collection. Raw-ECE differences of a few points between Jev and the baselines (Table~4 of the article) are therefore within the spread that rewording alone produces. For Qwen3-235B, the paraphrases also keep nDCG@10 within 2.3 points (DL19 68.6 versus 69.8--70.9; DL20 66.5 versus 65.5--66.6), but the original wording is the best calibrated: raw ECE rises from 18.6 to 20.6--30.4 on DL19 and from 22.0 to 25.3--34.9 on DL20. On SciFact (no original run) the paraphrases agree within 0.3 nDCG@10 and 1.6 ECE points. Paraphrase 3 and the last 6\% of paraphrase 2 were served by one deployment, after the other retired the model.

\subsection{Neural first stage}\label{app:first-stage}

To check whether the system ordering depends on BM25, we reranked RankLLM's SPLADE++ EnsembleDistil top-100 candidates for DL19 and DL20 (same qrels) with bge-reranker-v2-m3, MiniLM, Qwen3-Reranker-8B, and Jev's three formats. SPLADE++ alone reaches 73.1/72.0 nDCG@10 on DL19/DL20 (BM25: 50.6/48.0), so every reranker gains much less. bge-reranker-v2-m3 scores 76.0/75.6, Qwen8B 75.5/73.1, and MiniLM 74.9/74.4. Jev scores 70.5--72.9 on DL19, 3.1--5.5 points below bge (Score-4: $p=0.03$, unadjusted) and below the SPLADE++ ranking itself, whereas on BM25 candidates it was within 1.1 points of bge. On DL20 it scores 74.6--77.1, and Noul is the best system (77.1, $+1.5$ over bge, $p=0.50$). Jev, however, places more unjudged candidates in its top 10 than the cross-encoders do (Judged@10 86.7--87.9\% on DL19 versus 94.9\% for bge and 94.2--94.7\% for Qwen8B and MiniLM; 90.9--91.9\% versus 97.6\% for bge on DL20), and standard nDCG@10 counts them as non-relevant. On condensed lists, which drop unjudged candidates before scoring\cite{sakai2007alternatives}, Jev's DL19 gap to bge shrinks to $-0.1$ to $-1.7$ points (none significant), Jev is no longer below the SPLADE++ ranking (76.0--77.5 versus 75.9), and on DL20 it leads bge by $+3.0$ to $+4.0$ (Noul $p=0.04$, unadjusted). Condensed lists favor systems that surface unjudged candidates, so the two scorings only bracket the effect of incomplete judgments: the true DL19 gap lies somewhere between $-5.5$ and $-0.1$, and the DL19 deficit cannot be separated from incomplete judgments. The BM25 pools are also less complete for Jev (Judged@10 90.5--93.5\% versus 96.0--97.2\% for bge); there, condensed scoring moves Jev's DL19 difference to bge from $-1.0$ to $+0.5$ to between $+0.9$ and $+1.8$ points (not significant).

\subsection{Split and provenance audit}

Calibration, pilot, and final question IDs are disjoint, but MuSiQue families overlap substantially. Comparing \texttt{question\_decomposition[-1].id}, 494 final questions share the terminal sub-question with pilot, 349 with calibration, 265 with both, and 578 with either. Comparing any decomposition ID gives 593 pilot overlaps, 430 calibration overlaps, and 671 with either (352 with both). Counts are affected final questions, not unique IDs or matching pairs. The earlier whole-question normalization check found one pilot/final duplicate but missed component reuse. The local component audit checks the source hash and all six cached ranker split-ID sets; its CSV records question-level memberships. The 329-question sensitivity excludes any directly shared component, yet 23 questions in 18 source-graph families remain indirectly connected to design questions. It therefore cannot establish fully family-disjoint generalization. The audit and sensitivity scripts preserve the original splits and model outputs. The reproduction manifest records complete input SHA-256 hashes, all split IDs, installed package versions, analysis-source hashes, and the run-manifest hash, and links the component audit. Historical Hugging Face weight commits were not recorded; architecture strings cannot recover exact weight revisions. Original cached outputs should be preserved for historical numerical verification.
