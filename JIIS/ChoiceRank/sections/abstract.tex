Typed decision models are language models that return a probability for each option in a caller-defined answer set.
We test four such model families, without task-specific tuning, as pointwise rerankers of the top 100 BM25 candidates on 13 collections from TREC Deep Learning and BEIR.
The largest, Jev, averages 55.3--56.1 nDCG@10 (normalized discounted cumulative gain at rank 10) across three question formats, on par with a 4-bit Qwen3-Reranker-8B (56.0) and above the cross-encoder bge-reranker-v2-m3 (53.7), with wide variation across collections.
In same-base controls, Clef differs significantly from its base language model on one of seven collections, while Jeff improves on its base.
Jev's raw yes/no probabilities have lower expected calibration error than bge's (15.0 vs.\ 20.0), but bge calibrated on collections with the same relevance threshold closes this gap, and rewording the prompt moves raw calibration error by more than the gap.
In retrieval-augmented generation on a family-disjoint split of MuSiQue, Jev raises answer F1 over bge in all 27 policy--generator pairs (23 significant in family-clustered tests) and over Qwen3-Reranker-8B in 26 (12 significant); on Natural Questions, one of 27 differences is significant.
Probability thresholds do not beat fixed top-$k$ selection.
Typed decision models are competitive rerankers, but calibration alone does not improve context selection.
