\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Collections and candidates}

We use the candidates and relevance judgments of the RankGPT/RankLLM evaluation~\cite{sun2023rankgpt,rankllm} (our rerankers are pointwise, not listwise): TREC DL 2019--2023 passage~\cite{craswell2020dl19,craswell2021dl20,craswell2022dl21,craswell2023dl22,craswell2024dl23} (43/54/53/76/82 queries) and eight BEIR collections~\cite{thakur2021beir}---TREC-COVID, NFCorpus, Touche, DBPedia, SciFact, Signal, News, and Robust04 (308 DL and 1{,}525 BEIR queries, 1{,}833 in total; 15 NFCorpus queries have no BM25 candidates and score zero for every system).
Candidates are the Pyserini~\cite{pyserini} BM25 top-100 runs distributed with RankLLM, evaluated with the official Pyserini qrels; our BM25 nDCG@10 reproduces RankGPT's reported values on DL19/20 and seven of the eight BEIR collections (NFCorpus: 32.2 vs.\ 30.8).
Two caveats apply to the newer DL years: 31 of the 82 DL23 queries are synthetic (generated by T5 or GPT-4), and the DL22 judgments were propagated to near-duplicate passages; we follow common practice and keep both.
All systems rerank the full top-100 and are evaluated with \texttt{ir\_measures}~\cite{irmeasures}. nDCG@10 uses the graded judgments with linear gain; binary measures and calibration count grade $\ge2$ as relevant on TREC DL and grade $\ge1$ on BEIR (judged and unjudged counts per collection are in Online Resource~1, Table~S11). We use depth 100 because it is the depth at which RankGPT and RankLLM report results on these runs, and because the strong rerankers still improve between depths 50 and 100 on TREC DL (Online Resource~1, Table~S1).

\subsection{Systems}

Cross-encoders, both trained with MS~MARCO~\cite{bajaj2016msmarco} data: \texttt{ms-marco-MiniLM-L6-v2} (22M) and \texttt{bge-reranker-v2-m3} (568M, fp16)~\cite{bgererankerv2m3,chen2024bgem3}, both with a 512-token window; we rank by their logit $z$ and use $\sigma(z)$ as their raw probability.
Label-probability LLM rerankers, which read relevance from the probability of a ``yes'' token rather than from a typed answer: Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B, 4B, and 8B~\cite{qwen3embedding} with their official instruction template, ranking by the logit $\log P(\text{yes})-\log P(\text{no})$ and using its sigmoid, $P(\text{yes})$, as probability (4B and 8B with 4-bit NF4 weights~\cite{dettmers2023qlora} to fit an 8\,GB GPU), and Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507 (Qwen3 family~\cite{qwen3}) prompted pointwise for a Yes/No answer, with $P(\text{yes})$ renormalized over the Yes and No tokens among the top-5 next-token probabilities~\cite{zhuang2023beyond} (via OpenRouter; DL19--23, TREC-COVID, NFCorpus, and Robust04 only, for cost).
As a same-base control we ask Clef's base model, Qwen3.8-27B, the pointwise Yes/No prompt (Cloudflare's deployment via OpenRouter, reasoning disabled; on the seven collections where Clef was run), and Jeff\kern0pt's base model, Qwen3.5-0.8B (locally, thinking disabled, on all 13 collections; $P(\text{yes})$ from the full next-token distribution).
For Qwen3-235B and Jeff's base model we also use a graded readout~\cite{zhuang2023beyond}: the same prompt asks for a 0--4 rating, and passages are ranked by the expected rating over the digit tokens, renormalized over the digits present (for Qwen3-235B, among the top-5 tokens; at least one digit always appeared and all five in 90\% of judgments). All of these read the first 448 passage tokens, the cross-encoders' budget. Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B is close to bge-reranker-v2-m3 on average but significantly worse on DL22, DL23, and Touche and significantly better on TREC-COVID, NFCorpus, and Robust04.
Decision models, all used without task-specific tuning on our side: Laya (421M, ModernBERT-large~\cite{warner2024modernbert})~\cite{laya}; Jeff~\cite{jeff} (Jeff-Qwen3.5-0.8B v1.2); Jev~1.13 (snapshot \texttt{20260917}) via the OpenRouter Decisions API~\cite{jev}; and Clef~27B~\cite{clef}, an open-weight decision model adapted from Qwen3.8-27B, through Cloudflare's deployment on the same API (DL19--23, TREC-COVID, and Robust04 only, for cost).
Jev is a closed model whose training data we cannot inspect; MS~MARCO, BEIR, and Robust04 are old, public collections that may overlap with it.
For reference we also quote published results on the identical BM25 top-100 runs: monoT5-3B~\cite{nogueira2020monot5} and RankGPT~\cite{sun2023rankgpt}.

\subsection{Context budgets}

Models read different amounts of each passage: the cross-encoders 512 tokens including the query, Laya about 320 passage tokens, Jev, Clef, and Jeff the first 4{,}000 characters ($\approx$1{,}000 tokens), and the label-probability LLMs 448 passage tokens.
On TREC DL, DBPedia, and Signal almost no passage is truncated; on the BEIR abstract collections (TREC-COVID, NFCorpus, SciFact) 29--38\% exceed the cross-encoders' budget; and on Touche, News, and Robust04 69--97\% do (Online Resource~1, Table~S12).
As newer-content benchmark checks we add NovelEval-2306 (released with RankGPT~\cite{sun2023rankgpt}; 21 queries, 20 passages each, graded 0--2) and FutureQueryEval~\cite{abdallah2025futurequery} (148 queries about events after April 2025, graded 0--2, BM25 top-100 built with \texttt{bm25s}~\cite{lu2024bm25s} over its 2{,}787-document corpus); they are reported separately and not included in the 13-collection averages.
For the three long-document collections we add an equal-budget condition in which every passage is cut to its first 448 tokens before any model sees it, and bge-reranker-v2-m3 at a 2{,}048-token window. On these and the three abstract collections, Qwen3-Reranker-8B also reads the first 4{,}000 characters of each passage, as the decision models do.

\subsection{Metrics and tests}

Ranking: nDCG@10 with paired two-sided $t$-tests over queries; Holm correction~\cite{holm1979} covers the collections available for each system--baseline pair, not across the 75 system--baseline pairs. Table averages weight collections equally. We additionally pool collection effects with DerSimonian--Laird random effects~\cite{dersimonian1986} and report both normal inference and a post-hoc modified Knapp--Hartung sensitivity analysis~\cite{rover2015mkh}. mKH uses Student-$t$ with $k-1$ degrees of freedom and floors the residual variance multiplier at one. Pooled TOST~\cite{schuirmann1987tost} with a $\pm2$ nDCG margin is exploratory: the margin was chosen after seeing results, and normal/mKH conclusions are distinguished. We also report Judged@10 and condensed nDCG$'$@10~\cite{sakai2007alternatives} to assess sensitivity to incomplete pools.
Calibration: ECE (15 equal-width bins) and Brier score~\cite{brier1950} on judged pairs from nine collections with negatives (DL19--23, TREC-COVID, DBPedia, News, Robust04), with paired query-cluster bootstrap 95\% CIs (2,000 replicates; Holm over nine collections). Transfer Platt scaling uses no target labels: All pools the other collections with equal weight per pair; Group restricts sources to the same numeric binarization threshold and weights source collections equally (DL$\to$DL, dense BEIR$\to$BEIR). Numeric thresholds need not encode identical relevance semantics, especially for partial versus strict relevance in BEIR. In-collection Platt scaling uses target labels with 10$\times$ repeated five-fold query cross-fitting and serves as a reference floor.
Because judged pairs are not a random sample of candidates, we also report ECE when unjudged candidates are treated as non-relevant (Online Resource~1).

\subsection{RQ3: context selection}

We use MuSiQue-Ans dev~\cite{trivedi2022musique} (multi-hop; up to 20 candidate paragraphs per question, 2--4 labelled as supporting) and NQ-open test~\cite{kwiatkowski2019nq} with the DPR top-20 passages~\cite{karpukhin2020dpr} (single-hop; 18.6\% of final-set questions have no answer-bearing passage; gold labels come from DPR's string-match \texttt{has\_answer} flag).
With seed 13, each dataset is split into calibration (200), pilot (300), and final (1,000) questions with disjoint IDs. MuSiQue families were not kept together: 671 final questions share a decomposition sub-question ID with a design split.
For MuSiQue we therefore built a \emph{family-disjoint} split after the original analysis. Families are connected components of MuSiQue dev questions that share any decomposition ID; we drop every family that contains a pilot question and assign whole families to a calibration split (\texttt{calib\_fd}, 199 questions) and a final split (\texttt{final\_fd}, 597 questions in 270 families). Because the large 3- and 4-hop families reach the pilot, \texttt{final\_fd} is 88\% 2-hop (526/58/13 two-, three-, and four-hop questions, versus 52/31/17\% in the original final split). We reuse the nine policies and thresholds locked after the original pilot without re-tuning, and refit Platt scaling and Jev's readout on \texttt{calib\_fd} (construction details in Online Resource~1, \emph{Setup details}). The family-disjoint split is our primary MuSiQue result; the original split and the retrospective 329-question subset without directly shared sub-question IDs are reported as sensitivity analyses. NQ questions have no decompositions and keep the original split.
The calibration set is used to fit Platt scaling on gold labels and to choose Jev's probability readout by Brier score (Noul on MuSiQue, Choice-3 strict on NQ); thresholds are therefore applied to \emph{Platt-scaled} probabilities that use target-dataset labels.
Jev ranks candidates by its Score-4 expected level, bge-reranker-v2-m3 by its logit. After the final results we added, on the original and family-disjoint MuSiQue splits and on NQ, Qwen3-Reranker-8B (4-bit) and Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B, which rank by their yes/no logit (448-token passage budget, as in RQ1; MuSiQue and DPR passages are shorter); they receive the same nine policies, with Platt scaling fit on the same calibration questions. Policies select passages by fixed top-$k$, Adaptive-$k$ (cut after the largest gap between consecutive ranking scores~\cite{adaptivek2025}---Jev's Score-4 level or the bge or Qwen logit---taking the first of equal gaps, so $1\le k\le19$), a threshold $P>t$, or top-2 plus any further passage with $P>t$ (``top-2+threshold''). Ties in a ranking score keep the original candidate order. The nine policies compared between rankers are top-1/2/3/5, Adaptive-$k$, top-2+threshold at 0.3 and 0.5, and threshold at 0.2 and 0.3.
Three generators answer with the selected passages (temperature 0, at most 32 tokens, no option to abstain; prompt in Online Resource~1): Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct~\cite{qwen25}, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct~\cite{llama3}, and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507, via OpenRouter. We score answers with SQuAD-normalized F1~\cite{rajpurkar2016squad}.
Policies and thresholds were fixed after the pilot before final generation. The locked threshold-rule comparisons were against top-3 and Adaptive-$k$; comparisons against top-2 and top-5 were added later and are exploratory. The pilot motivated the top-2+threshold floor. Raw thresholds (MuSiQue pilot Qwen2.5-7B: 41.6 F1 at 0.3 versus 43.9 Platt-scaled) were dropped from the lock and added after final results as a post-hoc analysis. Their probability readout still uses labelled calibration questions, so they avoid Platt fitting but are not label-free.
Comparisons are paired over questions. For each dataset and generator, Holm correction is applied separately to the nine ranker-effect tests (Jev vs.\ bge-reranker-v2-m3 under each policy) and to the four threshold-rule tests (top-2+threshold at 0.3 vs.\ top-2, top-3, top-5, and Adaptive-$k$); an earlier version of our analysis mixed the two families, and we separated them after an internal review. We add 95\% CIs and, after seeing the final results, TOST equivalence tests with a $\pm2$ F1 margin, which we report as post-hoc and without multiplicity correction.
Question-level tests do not account for dependence between questions of the same MuSiQue family. Our primary MuSiQue tests are therefore family cluster-robust paired $t$-tests (CR1, $G-1$ degrees of freedom for $G$ families), Holm-corrected over the nine policies as above; question-level tests, a family sign-flip randomization test, and family-bootstrap 95\% CIs are sensitivity analyses (Online Resource~1, \emph{Setup details}). NQ questions have no families, so NQ keeps question-level tests.

\subsection{Hardware}

Local models ran on a laptop RTX~4060 (8\,GB); Jeff ran through its reference server, whose attention kernels fall back to a slow reference implementation on Windows, so its latency is pessimistic.
API models were called through OpenRouter; Jev received all three questions per call. All API calls in this study cost about \$55 in total, so every experiment can be reproduced on a small budget.
OpenRouter routed the Llama and Qwen3-30B generators across several hosting providers (Qwen2.5-7B was served by one), which may serve differently quantized weights; provider assignment was roughly similar across policies within a run, but the Qwen3-Reranker contexts were generated later with a different provider mix, so comparisons with them also differ in serving provider; we record the provider of every call.
